A desk can hold a bright blue pencil, a yellow notebook, and a very ordinary sheet of paper. The color does not have to fill the whole room to make the room feel different.

Maybe an interface can work the same way.

## A few colors, with jobs to do

In this small design study, blue is a point of curiosity. Red marks an interruption. Yellow is an object you might pick up. Green brings the pace down a little.

Those are creative choices, not universal meanings. Their usefulness depends on what surrounds them.

> Give the eye somewhere to wander. Then give it somewhere to rest.

## Fast at the edges, slow in the middle

The cover of a notebook can be playful. Once it is open, the page has a simpler job: help a thought make its way from one person to another.

A short checklist for this imaginary notebook:

- [x] Let the cover carry some personality.
- [x] Keep the reading column comfortable.
- [x] Give controls clear names.
- [ ] Stop adding details when they stop adding meaning.

## An unfinished thought

I like the tension between an orderly page and a slightly crooked object. One says: you can find your way around. The other says: a person was here.

Perhaps the right amount of personality is the amount that makes you want to stay, without making it harder to read.
