Language models invite an old metaphor: the **automaton** — gears arranged just so, until speech appears to spill out of brass.

The metaphor is useful until it isn't. Pretraining is less a single machine and more a long correspondence between data, compute, and inductive bias. What looks like wit is often compressed literature. What looks like reasoning is often path-finding through a landscape of next tokens.

## Three letters from the training run

1. **Compression is not understanding** — but it is a stubborn prerequisite.
2. **Taste** shows up in data mixtures long before it shows up in chat.
3. **Scale** rearranges failure modes; it does not abolish them.

If LLMs are portable magic, the spellbook is still half footnotes: tokenization quirks, long-context decay, and the quiet politics of what was never in the corpus.

I keep a postage-stamp sketch of a clockwork head above my desk — not because models are machines in the Victorian sense, but because the craft still rewards patience, measurement, and a little romance about minds made of math.
