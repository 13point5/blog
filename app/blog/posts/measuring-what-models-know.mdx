Evaluation is the least glamorous part of the stack and the part that decides whether anything else was real.

A leaderboard is a set of balance scales left in a drafty hall. Tip the dataset, tip the prompt template, tip the judge model, and the brass pans swing. That does not make measurement useless — it makes **protocol** the craft.

## A small ledger

| Temptation | Better habit |
| --- | --- |
| One number to rule them all | A portfolio of tasks with known failure modes |
| "The model got 92%" | "On this split, with this rubric, under this contamination check" |
| Autopilot LLM-as-judge | Spot-check humans + adversarial cases |
| Celebrate the bump | Ask what capability actually moved |

Good evals feel like calipers: slightly unromantic, slightly severe, oddly intimate with the thing being measured. Bad evals feel like applause.

Keep the scales in the stamp. Keep the footnotes longer than the headline.
