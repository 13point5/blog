I like to read architectures the way one reads an old elevation drawing: columns, arches, load paths.

Transformers made a cathedral of attention — parallel, wide, and hungry for context. Mixture-of-experts added side chapels. State-space models whispered about longer naves with fewer stained-glass parameters. None of these drawings are the building; they are invitations to stress-test where the weight actually sits.

## What the blueprint hides

Architecture diagrams flatten:

- **Training recipe** (the scaffolding that never makes the brochure)
- **Tokenizer and context policy** (the foundation grade)
- **Serving constraints** (the weather the building must endure)

When people say "we swapped the arch," they often mean they swapped a story. The useful question is narrower: what failure did this elevation make rarer, and what new crack did it introduce under load?

Stamp the drawing. Then run the evals.
