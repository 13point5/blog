The most useful part of a lesson is sometimes the question you carry out of it.

This is an imagined notebook entry, a place to try the book-shaped cover and a quieter reading layout.

## What stayed with you?

Suppose two people finish the same tutorial. One remembers the steps. The other forgets a step but remembers why it was necessary.

Neither observation is enough to declare who learned more. We need to know what happens when the task changes.

> A good explanation leaves you with something you can use somewhere else.

## A small exercise

1. Pick a concept you encountered today.
2. Explain it without using the name of the concept.
3. Invent a new example.
4. Write down the part you still cannot explain.

<details>
<summary>A question for later</summary>

When you get stuck, is the missing piece a fact, a connection, or a chance to try it yourself?

</details>

## Keep the margin open

The blank space beside an explanation is an invitation to disagree, draw, connect, and return. This little book is for those unfinished thoughts.
